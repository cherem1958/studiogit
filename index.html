    THE RECORD BEHIND THE NUMBER
    TRANSPARENCY BY DESIGN

    Sources & methodology.

    Economic observations come from the Central Bank of Brazil’s SGS API. IPCA is produced by IBGE; the CDI reference originates from B3. Select an indicator to inspect its exact series, history and dates.

    • Daily checks, different releases. The dashboard checks economic sources every hour while open and on return to the page. With the local server running, the backend also checks in the background. Exchange rates and CDI follow business-day publications; IPCA is monthly.
    • Dates mean different things. “As of” is the observation date or month. “Checked” is the time of the last successful fetch. Old observations are flagged separately from a failed refresh.
    • Changes are neutral. FX changes are percentages versus the previous published observation. Interest-rate and inflation changes are percentage points. A rise is not presented as good or bad.
    • Small charts, honest scope. Each sparkline shows up to twelve published observations, ordered by date, with its own vertical scale. They are not comparable in magnitude. Missing values are never filled with estimates.
    • When a source fails. The last successfully checked economic data may be shown for up to seven days, explicitly marked as cached. Otherwise the value is unavailable. Weather observations older than six hours are not shown.
    • Weather. Belo Horizonte conditions are model estimates from Open-Meteo ↗, checked every fifteen minutes while open. They are not readings from a specific local station.
    • Your data. Tasks are stored locally, not sent to the data providers or GitHub. Export/import transfers backups; there is no automatic cross-device task sync.
